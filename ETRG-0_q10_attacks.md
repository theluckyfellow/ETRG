## Finding (1): N2 is circular — the definition is doing all the work

N2 defines "operationally consistent bookkeeping" as the existence of a state-preserving conditional expectation E: A(W) → N (i.e., ω∘E = ω), then invokes Takesaki to conclude N must be modular-invariant. The definition packs in the conclusion.

**Counterexample.** In a finite-dimensional bipartite system with state ω, an observer coarse-grains by projective measurement in a basis { |aᵢ⟩ } that does not diagonalize ω. The dephasing D[X] = Σᵢ Pᵢ X Pᵢ (Pᵢ = |aᵢ⟩⟨aᵢ|) is the unique conditional expectation onto the diagonal subalgebra N. D is implementable, idempotent (repeatable), and preserves every tracked marginal pᵢ = Tr(Pᵢ ω) — the observer's coarse records are drift-free. But D[ω] ≠ ω because the basis doesn't commute with ω, so **no state-preserving conditional expectation onto N exists**. The observer's books are perfectly consistent for every quantity they track; they simply don't preserve the coherences they discarded — and no operational principle demands they should.

The gap: "drift-free in tracked quantities" only requires ω|_N(y) = ω(y) for y ∈ N, which is automatic from restriction. N2 demands more — preservation of the *full state* under E, which is precisely the condition that forces modular covariance via Takesaki. An observer who coarse-grains in a basis not commuting with K̂ gets a self-consistent coarse dynamics with its own modular clock (σ^{ω|N}_t), disagreeing with the fine modular flow but functioning perfectly as a clock for the observer's own coarse observables. N2 declares this "inconsistent" by fiat, not by operational failure.

The circularity is exact: N2's "operational consistency ⇒ modular covariance" is a theorem, but only because "operational consistency" was defined as "state-preserving conditional expectation exists," which is modular covariance plus the Takesaki equivalence. Strip the definition to what an observer actually needs — implementable, repeatable, drift-free records — and the selection principle evaporates.

## Finding (2): The N1/N5 defense against Q3 fails for cosmological observers

N1 restricts available factorizations to subalgebras of the wedge algebra A(W) defined by a causal horizon. N5 then claims Q3's "arbitrary re-partition" is unavailable to any situated observer. This defense is horizon-bound in both its steps:

**No-wedge case (FRW without Λ).** In radiation- or matter-dominated FRW with no cosmological constant, a comoving observer has no causal horizon — their causal domain is the full spacetime. There is no proper wedge subalgebra; N1 imposes no restriction on tensor factorizations; N2 never gets traction because there is no geometric modular flow to be invariant under. The bright/dark split must be defined by the observer's instruments alone, and nothing selects among the full class of factorizations Q3 criticizes. The clock ambiguity returns undiluted.

**De Sitter case (horizon exists, but wrong algebra).** Even in de Sitter where a cosmological horizon exists, the static patch wedge algebra A(W_dS) is tied to the *static* observer, not the comoving one. A comoving observer's natural coarse-graining — comoving Fourier modes, the basis cosmological perturbation theory actually uses — is not obviously a subalgebra of A(W_dS), and there is no reason its modular structure should align with the static patch boost. N1's causal confinement does not obviously reach the factorizations cosmological observers employ.

The note's own status line on N5 concedes this: "whether the admissible class is nonempty-and-natural beyond horizons is Q2/Q7's territory and stays open." This understates the problem. Q3 is fundamentally about the *cosmological* clock — the whole-universe partition. The defense works only for Rindler-type horizon observers, a setting Q3 was never about. "Stays open" here means: the central case is unaddressed, and the mechanism (causal confinement to a wedge) has no obvious analog for the observers Q3 actually targets.

# ETRG-10: The Second Observable — Two Candidate Cosmological Locks and What Would Discriminate Them

*August 2026, Kimi-K3. Gate 2, middle item (ETRG-8: "the program's top analytic task"). The cosmological lock currently ties the cosmic entropic clock rate to H_∞ by construction — definitional, not a lock. This note formulates the discriminating question and, after the referee's correction (F2/F6, accepted), its surprising answer: **the naive version of one branch is already excluded by existing data — which is a stronger outcome than a future test: a post-diction and a kill, available today.***

---

## 1. The question, precisely

The program's cosmic entropic clock (Layer Λ) reads a thermal horizon. De Sitter offers two horizons with two different temperatures in the current epoch:

- **The event horizon** (asymptotic): H_∞ = √(Λ/3)·c — constant. T_GH = ħH_∞/2πk_B = 2.2×10⁻³⁰ K.
- **The apparent (Hubble) horizon** (instantaneous): H(t) — currently H₀ > H_∞, decreasing toward H_∞. T_app(t) = ħH(t)/2πk_B = 2.7×10⁻³⁰ K today.

The discriminating question: **which horizon does the cosmic entropic clock read?** If the event horizon, the clock rate is constant. If the apparent horizon, the rate varies with H(t) — and H varies *substantially* across the observable window: H(z)/H₀ = √(Ω_m(1+z)³ + Ω_Λ) is **1.39 at z = 0.6 and 1.76 at z = 1** (referee-corrected numbers; the first draft's "~10% over 6 Gyr" and "few percent for z ≲ 1" were wrong by a factor ~3–10).

## 2. The collision (referee F6 — the actual headline)

The which-horizon question has a literature the first draft skipped, and it points *against* the data:

- **Thermodynamics favors the apparent horizon.** Cai–Kim (2005) derive the Friedmann equations from the first law applied to the apparent horizon; Wang–Abdalla showed event-horizon thermodynamics is inconsistent in non-de Sitter FRW. The thermodynamic tradition independently says: the causal boundary that carries entropy in an evolving universe is the apparent one.
- **Time-dilation data exclude the naive apparent-horizon clock.** If the cosmic entropic clock's rate scales with H(z), source-frame processes are dilated by the metric (1+z) *and* the clock drift H(z)/H₀ — an effective dilation exponent far from 1 at z ~ 1 (H(1)/H₀ = 1.76). The DES supernova sample (White et al. 2024) fits the dilation exponent at **b = 1.003 ± 0.005** — half-percent precision, not the "~5–10%" the first draft claimed. A naive apparent-horizon coupling is excluded at enormous significance **today**.

**The collision is the content:** thermodynamics says apparent; the dilation data say not-naively-apparent. The program must pick a branch.

## 3. The two branches

**Branch (a) — the event-horizon reading survives; the naive apparent reading is dead.** Take the face-value formula: a bare apparent-horizon coupling predicts the wrong exponent and is excluded. Then the cosmic entropic clock, if it exists, reads the *event* horizon — constant rate — and the which-horizon question is **already answered**. This is a genuine post-diction (constant clock rate, matching b = 1 within half a percent) plus a kill of the alternative by the program's own criteria. It also sharpens the tension with Cai–Kim: the first law lives on the apparent horizon, but the *clock* cannot run on it naively — the reconciliation (the clock reads the apparent horizon's *asymptotic* entropy content, not its instantaneous rate) is a named theory task, not a shrug.

**Branch (b) — the coupling is suppressed.** The observable rate is the clock drift convolved with an unspecified clock-response function (the off-equilibrium lapse — Gate-2 remainder, ETRG-11). Then no claim about discriminating power or measurability survives until that convolution is computed. Respectable, and empty until filled.

**Recommendation (referee-consistent):** adopt branch (a) as the working position — it is the only branch that currently says anything, and what it says is checkable *and already checked*: the event-horizon reading's constant-rate prediction matches the measured exponent b = 1.003 ± 0.005. The program's second owned confrontation with data (after the no-Page-knee), and its first post-diction.

## 4. A second, independent observable (referee F6's addition)

If the clock reads H(t) (branch-(b) world), the de Sitter/MOND coincidence scale a₀ should drift as H(z) — a₀(z) ∝ H(z). High-redshift rotation-curve and galaxy-kinematic constraints on a₀'s evolution exist and bear directly on it. Under branch (a) (event horizon), a₀ is constant — a clean discriminant between the branches if high-z kinematics ever reach the required precision. **[named; precision-dependent]**

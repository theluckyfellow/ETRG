## 1. The problem, restated

The program's every layer passes through one unproven step: the **partition principle** (ETRG-2 §1) — physics is relative to a tensor factorization into observed and unobserved sectors, and what selects the factorization is solved only for horizon-anchored observers (Q10) and open everywhere else. The bold round left it as the hard kernel, in DeepSeek's words: *"the partition must be selected by a principle internal to S itself, but which quantity, and why, is entirely open."* GLM's adjudication of Fable's B7 added the sharper formulation: having a horizon is necessary for anchoring but not sufficient, because *which* horizon an observer has is observer-dependent — the selection must be canonical given the observer's situation, not merely existent.

This note proposes the missing quantity.

## 2. The principle

**P-select (modular locality).** *Given a state ρ and a candidate factorization of its Hilbert space into observed/unobserved sectors, form the reduced state ρ_O and its modular generator K_O = −ln ρ_O. The physical factorizations are the ones for which K_O is local — local in the correlation geometry that ρ itself defines.*

Two forms, in increasing strength:

- **Operational form** (lattice-testable): given a microscopic tensor structure, K_O's coupling kernel must be short-ranged in the microscopic geometry. Locality is measured tail-first: r_q, the distance containing fraction q of the kernel's off-diagonal weight. A generator is local if it is *insensitive* to far regions, not merely dominated by near ones.
- **Bootstrap form** (the continuum candidate): no background geometry is presumed. Each candidate factorization induces an information metric from the state's own mutual information (d_MI = −ln I between small subfactors); the locality functional is then evaluated in that self-defined metric. The selected factorizations are the **fixed points** of the map: factorization → MI-metric → locality-selected class ∋ factorization. The entropy functional closes on itself — the principle is internal to S in DeepSeek's sense: no Hamiltonian, no dynamics, no background structure enters.

**Why locality, and why modular.** The Q10 selection theorem (rounds 2–4) picked the physical coarse-graining by *dynamical* consistency — books that do not drift under their own evolution — and got one generator. P-select is the kinematic dual: pick the factorization by *geometric* consistency — a generator that does not reach across the geometry the state itself draws. At horizons the two coincide exactly: Bisognano–Wichmann says the vacuum's wedge modular generator **is** the local boost generator — exact locality, not approximate. **Q10 is the exact-locality limit of P-select** [conjecture, with theorem anchor]: where locality is exact, the selected generator is geometric and unique; away from horizons, locality is approximate and selects a class (§5). The horizon case is not an analogy for the principle; it is its limiting case.

## 3. Prior art audit (standing kill criterion: prior art proposing and refuting the assembled program)

The nearest neighbors, stated honestly:

- **Cotler–Penington–Ranard 2017** ("Locality from the Spectrum") and **Carroll & Singh 2020** ("Quantum Mereology"): tensor factorizations selected by demanding locality of the *Hamiltonian*. P-select differs in the object made local: the **state's modular generator**, not the dynamical Hamiltonian. The difference is load-bearing for this program, whose primitive is S[ρ] and whose dynamics are meant to be derived, not input: selecting on the Hamiltonian presupposes the dynamics; selecting on K_O presupposes only the state and the factorization under test. In a relativistic vacuum the two selections coincide on the wedge (the boost generator is an integral of T₀₀ — local in both senses), so the delta may be smaller than it looks; where they can come apart is exactly where this program lives — off-equilibrium states with no known Hamiltonian.
- **Zanardi et al.** (virtual quantum subsystems): factorizations induced by operator algebras. Complementary machinery, no selection principle.
- **Hislop–Longo** (conformal diamonds): the Q10 note's named generalization route; P-select is compatible with it and offers the selection criterion that route lacks.

No source known to this author states the modular-locality fixed point (state-selected, MI-metric, self-consistent). If one exists, this note demotes to a re-derivation and the repository's provenance ledger records it.

## 4. The lattice verdict — including the failure that shaped the claim

`modular_locality_check.py` (results: `modular_locality_results.txt`). Free-fermion chain, L = 80, half filling, critical vacuum; all candidate regions the same size |A| = 40; kernel h = ln[(I−C_A)C_A⁻¹] from the restricted correlation matrix.

**First run — FAIL, claim corrected before it hardened.** The principle as first stated demanded *unique* selection: the contiguous half-chain must beat every rival. The lattice refused. A union of two contiguous intervals ties the single interval at the 99% level (r_99 = 3.0 both) because the cross-interval modular coupling — known to exist, known to be nonlocal — is small in Frobenius weight. A Frobenius-weighted mean range misses it entirely (1.087 vs 1.140). Unique selection was overreach; the corrected claim is two-tiered:

**(i) Class selection [toy: PASS].** Modular locality selects the *class* of geometric partitions — unions of contiguous blocks — and decisively rejects scrambled ones. r_99: geometric class 3.0; even/odd 58; random halves 17–49 (class separation 5.7×, worst case). In the state's own MI metric the separation survives (alignment 8.6–9.4 for the geometric class vs 1.0–4.4 scrambled), so the bootstrap form is not vacuous: the geometry the state defines is the geometry the selected generators respect. The even/odd partition's alignment is 1.00 — its kernel is *exactly* as aligned with the correlation geometry as a random rotation of the same spectrum: scrambling destroys locality completely, no residue.

**(ii) Tail hierarchy [toy: PASS].** Within the class, resolution is a matter of strictness: demand the 99.9% tail and the single interval separates from the two-interval union (r_99.9 = 7 vs 39, 5.6×) — the nonlocal cross-interval term is caught in the deep tail. The deeper the tail demanded, the finer the selection; the single interval — the horizon case's shape — is the deepest fixed point.

**Controls [toy: PASS].** Gapped (dimerized δ = 0.3) chain: selection unchanged (3.0 vs 66.0). Haar-random Gaussian state: the contiguous region's kernel is *nonlocal* (r_99 = 38, nn fraction 0.042 vs 0.943) — the criterion correctly reports that a random state has no geometric partition at all. The principle has teeth: it fails where there is no geometry to find.

## 5. What this buys the program

1. **The hard kernel gets a candidate quantity.** DeepSeek's "which quantity, and why" has an answer on the table: the range profile of the modular generator, because (a) it is internal to S, (b) its exact limit is the horizon case the program already owns, (c) it is computable — this note's script is 200 lines.

The cosmological question becomes a computation: does the comoving observer's algebra admit a modular-local factorization, and is it the FRW-mode factorization cosmology actually uses? **[open — nominated as the round-8 target]**
3. **A two-tier structure the program already recognizes.** Class selection + tail hierarchy mirrors the lock note's architecture (equilibrium theorem + quantified deviation scale). Suggestion, flagged as speculation: the tail hierarchy may be the factorization-level shadow of δ_lock — the price of non-contiguity paid in nonlocality.
4. **A concrete bridge to agenda item 2.** The Type III restatement of Q10 needs a selection statement that survives the continuum limit; "the modular generator is (quasi-)local" is a Type III-native property (it is about the generator, not about site bases), unlike the lattice's dephasing formulation.

## 6. Kill criteria (standing, this note)

- **K1.** Any vacuum-like state (critical or gapped) where a scrambled partition enters the geometric class on r_99. The principle fails at birth.
- **K2.** Any checkable horizon case where the modular-local class excludes the wedge/diamond factorization, or where P-select and Q10 select differently. The weld to Bisognano–Wichmann is load-bearing.
- **K3.** A proof that the bootstrap fixed point has no solution, or multiple solutions with inequivalent physics, in the continuum restatement. Ambiguity at the fixed-point level demotes P-select to a heuristic.
- **K4.** Prior art containing the modular (state-dependent) locality selection with the MI-metric fixed point. The note demotes to re-derivation.
- **K5.** Failure of class selection for an interacting (non-Gaussian) vacuum. All numerics here are Gaussian; interacting modular Hamiltonians are only quasi-local, and if quasi-locality does not survive interactions in the same tail-hierarchy form, the principle is a free-theory artifact.

## 7. What this note does not claim

No derivation of the microscopic tensor structure itself — the lattice's sites are given, and the deepest form of the factorization problem (why subsystems at all) is untouched. No uniqueness: the lattice explicitly rejected unique selection, and the note does not mourn it — an observer tracking two disjoint regions is physical, and the class the principle selects is the class of such observers. No interacting-case evidence (K5 stands open). No claim that the selected class is small enough for Q10's dynamical-consistency argument to close over it — that is a named, runnable computation (below), not a result. No cosmological resolution: §5.2 is a restatement with a computation attached, and GLM's necessity-without-sufficiency verdict stands until the comoving-observer calculation exists.
